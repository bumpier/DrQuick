DrQuick logo, round two

All three build on the switch from round one and add the one thing it lacked: a sign that says doctor. Each is shown as an app icon, as a favicon at actual size and in one colour. The usage guide covers the recommended option, 1.

Usage guide

What changed

Round one's switch said on demand but not doctor, and the Q and dq marks sat off-centre. Both of those are retired. Every option now pairs the switch with a plus and keeps the balanced, horizontal pill.

The brief

  • PromiseA GP on video within minutes of asking.
  • FeelQuick, warm, confident. Never clinical, never NHS.
  • Must work16px favicon, app icon, one colour, reversed on forest.
  • AvoidCrosses, stethoscopes, heartbeat lines, shields, clocks, lightning bolts, NHS blue and green.

Why the logo doesn't show surge pricing

Demand pricing belongs in the product, where the price is shown in full and held. Putting it in the logo would make price the brand. The ASA has ruled time-pressure pricing in medical ads socially irresponsible, and "charges more when you're sickest" is the headline critics will reach for. All three marks carry speed and availability instead.

1 Plus switch

Wordmark + symbolRecommended

Switch on a doctor. The knob of the lime switch now carries a plus, the universal sign for care.

  • Keeps what you liked: the lime pill from the site and the current wordmark, with the switch in the on position.
  • The plus says doctor at a glance, and the switch around it says on demand. Together they tell the whole story.
  • Below 24px the knob works alone, so the favicon is a clean plus in a circle.

Risk. The plus is common across health brands. What makes this ownable is the switch around it, so use the full symbol wherever there is room.

3 Switch cross

Symbol + wordmark

A medical plus built from two switch tracks, with the knob at the on end of one arm.

  • The most immediately medical of the three. It reads as health even without the name.
  • Square, so it makes the strongest app icon and avatar.
  • Works alone in app stores and on social profiles.

Risk. A green cross is the UK pharmacy sign, which can suggest a prescription service, and the product rules forbid implying that a consultation leads to one. The knob also reads as a hole at small sizes.

Usage guide

Option 1 · Plus switch

One page for anyone placing the logo. If you choose option 2 or 3, the same rules apply with that option's files.

Versions

Primary lockupThe default wherever there is room: site header, email, documents, signage.
SymbolOnly where the name is already present or the space is square: app icon, favicon, avatars.
One colourForest, black or white, with "Quick" and the knob knocked out and the plus kept solid. For print, embroidery, stamps and lime grounds.

Clear space

Keep at least x clear on every side, where x is the knob's radius. No text, edge or other logo enters that box.

App icon and favicon

  • App icon: a forest tile with the lime switch at 76% of its width. Submit the full-bleed square app-icon-ios.svg at 1024px; iOS and Android apply their own corner mask.

  • Favicon: the knob alone, a forest disc with a lime plus, on a lime tile. Ship favicon.svg and favicon.ico (16, 32 and 48px).

  • Apple touch icon: png/app-icon-180.png.

Type

The wordmark is Plus Jakarta Sans ExtraBold (800) at −0.04em tracking, converted to outlines. Set headlines in the same face at 800 and body text at 400, as on the site. The font's SIL Open Font License allows logo use.

Files

Each option lives in Brand/logo/r1-plus-knob/, r3-plus-on/ or r2-switch-cross/. Each folder holds the pill wordmark (options 1 and 2), the symbol and a symbol-plus-name lockup as SVGs in colour, forest, black and white, a reversed lockup, app-icon.svg, app-icon-ios.svg, favicon.svg and favicon.ico, with PNGs in png/. The sources that rebuild everything are in Brand/logo/src/.

Before you commit to an option, have a trademark attorney run a clearance search. I checked these marks by eye against the category, not against the trademark registers.
