# Dr Quick — Website

> **React migration in progress** (milestone 1 done — see
> `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-28-react-migration-design.md`). The site now
> builds from the Next.js app here: `npm run dev` / `npm test` /
> `npm run typecheck` / `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL=… npm run build`. The landing
> page is `app/page.tsx` + `app/landing-content.tsx`; the API lives in
> `app/api/`; headers live in `next.config.ts`. The old `index.html` and
> `preview/` remain as reference until milestone 4 and are no longer served.

Pre-launch website for Dr Quick: on-demand private GP video consultations (England). A landing page that opens on the GP sign-up (four details and a one-off fee paid through Stripe) and carries the patient waitlist (an email) behind the switch, six supporting pages, and a blog written by admins. Approved GPs sign in to a doctor portal at `/doctor` (see Doctor portal). No patient accounts, no health data, no third-party tracking: first-party analytics only, identified only with consent (see Analytics).

## Design rules — non-negotiable

- **Keep it simple, stupid.** No unneeded text, no eyebrow labels, no section labels, no fine-print paragraphs. Every element must earn its place. When in doubt, delete.
- **Palette:** the `DESIGN.md` front matter ("Lime & Forest", adopted 2026-09-25, replacing TechMed Modern) is the source of truth. `app/globals.css` declares it and `tests/constraints.test.ts` enforces it:
  - Ground `#F5F7F2` (surface). Cards and fields are true white `#FFFFFF`. Quiet tile and hover `#EDF1E8`; switch track `#E8EDE3`, hover `#DFE6D8`; disabled `#6B7A63`; hairlines and field borders `#DCE4D4`.
  - Ink is forest `#163300`, the same dark as the fills (the band). Secondary text `#4D5B45`; on the band `#B5C9A5`. Never true black.
  - **Lime `#9FE870` is a fill, never a line**: every primary button, selected state and "you are here" mark, always carrying forest text. It is 1.4:1 on white, so it is never text, a stroke, a ring or a chart mark on a light surface — that is **`primary-ink` `#2F6B0F`**. Hover `#8BDB57`. On the band, lime is the accent and may be text.
  - Bento tile tones: `lime-wash` `#E2F6D5`, `stone` `#E8E5DB`, `sage` `#C9DDB8`. Greens and warm neutrals only: the user retired yellow and peach tones on 2026-09-25 because they read feminine; do not reintroduce warm pastels.
  - Semantic: success `#2F6B0F`, error `#C8322A` — the status icons, the invalid field and the 999 icon only. Never decoration.
  - Never NHS Blue `#005EB8` or NHS Green `#009639`, or anything near either. Dr Quick is a private provider and must never read as NHS-branded. The retired TechMed (`#0047FF`) and true-black / `#1447E6` systems are not to be reintroduced.
- **Typography:** Plus Jakarta Sans only (400–800, `next/font`, system-ui fallback). Headlines 800 at -0.035em, h3 and titles 700, reading copy 16px, leads 17–21px, fine print 13px.
- **Layout: bento.** The landing nav = wordmark + the page links (from 1080px) + role switch + one lime CTA, sticky; every other page uses `SiteNav` (wordmark, the four page links, one lime CTA to `/#join`, a sheet menu below 900px). Every section is rounded tiles on the ground, 16px apart — colour separates, not hairlines. Tiles must vary in span, tone and internal layout; a row of identical cards is a failure. Both heroes are the same shape — a white tile with headline + capture, beside the `HeroTiles` photo cluster — and that symmetry is what makes the equal-billing decision read visually; do not give one mode a different hero skeleton. In how-it-works, the forest fill belongs to step three, the payoff.
- **Breakpoints:** three, and they are not interchangeable. `1080px` collapses only the two form-bearing grids (below it their columns squeeze the email input narrower than a phone gets). `900px` is the general two-column-to-one. `560px` is the phone pass. Spacing is a 4-unit base — see the scale in `DESIGN.md`.
- **Two modes, one page, and it opens on GPs** (the user's decision, 2026-10-01; `DEFAULT_ROLE` in `lib/site-mode.ts`). The nav carries a GPs / Patients segmented control. Both modes ship in the DOM, the GP one first; the inline head script resolves the role before paint from `?role=patient` or `?role=gp`, failing that from a hash that belongs to one mode (`#join` / `#join2` for patients, `#gps` / `#gp-join` for GPs), failing that the default, and `.js[data-role]` hides the other. Without JS neither hiding rule matches and both modes render stacked — never make the switch the only way to reach either audience's content. The bare address is the GP page and `?role=patient` is the link to send a patient; switching updates it with `replaceState`, scrolls to top and moves focus to the new `h1`. The head (title, description, share text) describes the default mode; `assets/og.png` is still the patient card and needs redrawing in `../Branding/src/site.py`.
- **Section order** (changing it changes what the reader knows when). Patient: hero → 999 band → how it works → what it covers and doesn't → price → FAQ → closing capture. GP: hero → how a shift works → what Dr Quick does and doesn't → what you're paid → FAQ → closing capture. The two run in parallel on purpose: expectation-setting sits before the money in both, so the price promise and the pay promise are each read against a known scope. The footer is shared.
- **Motion:** content arrives with one shared grammar (a 16px rise; the headline by masked line), staggered 70ms and capped at 300ms. Mark new content `data-reveal` and wrap sibling groups in `[data-stagger]` so it inherits that grammar rather than inventing a second one. Reveal start-states live under `.js` so a failed script never blanks the page.
- **Craft bar: Wise / HelloFresh.** Bold flat colour blocks, friendly round type, bento tiles, big pill buttons. Chosen by the user on 2026-09-25 over the category canon ("Uber", TechMed): the brief is to inspire, build trust and look new — not clinical, not NHS. Warmth never outruns honesty: no invented stats, faces, counts or testimonials.
- **Both audiences get a full page**, not a section; the GP one is the page the site opens on. The GP mode's forest tile is the pay tile — the dark fill sits on the payoff, which for a GP is what they keep: the commission ladder (`CommissionLadder`, drawn from `lib/finance/commission.ts`), 60% rising to 75%. The 999 band is patient-flow content and appears in patient mode only; the footer instance is shared, so the page never loses it.
- **No gradients, no glass, no gradient text, no emoji or unicode icons.** The one exception: `linear-gradient` may draw the hard-edged 1px lime line grid (`.band-grid`) on forest fills — never a visible colour transition and never a wash. Icons are authored SVG. Elevation is flat: white tiles carry a 1px forest ring at 6% (`shadow-card`), tinted tiles carry nothing, only floating layers (`shadow-pop`) lift, and fields get the 4px lime glow on focus. No coloured or decorative shadows.
- **Imagery: photography.** The landing page's image slots are `PhotoTile`s; until real photographs arrive they show a tone, a hard-edged disc and a line glyph, and never stock people. `docs/photo-brief.md` is the shot list and its rules (nothing that passes for a real Dr Quick GP, no medicine, no NHS or CQC branding). No Storyset asset ships any more, so the Storyset attribution is gone; if one ever returns, its licence link must return to the footer with it.

## Component layer

- **`components/ui/` is shadcn/ui** (Radix base, the unified `radix-ui` package), installed with the CLI (`npx shadcn@latest init -d --base radix` — the default picks Base UI, which is not this layer) and then restyled to `DESIGN.md`. Every button, field, card, tab, table, dialog and badge on the landing page and on the dashboards is one of these components reading the same tokens. Add components via the CLI, then restyle — never hand-write a primitive that shadcn already provides, and never install one no screen needs yet. `components.json` is the CLI's config; `lib/utils.ts` exports `cn()` with this project's named scales registered in tailwind-merge, so a `className` override such as `p-tile-lead-pad` actually displaces the component's own class instead of leaving the cascade to decide.
- **The token mapping lives in `DESIGN.md`** ("shadcn token mapping"). Every shadcn semantic variable (`background`, `card`, `muted-foreground`, `border`, `input`, `ring` …) is an alias of an existing `@theme` colour, declared in `@theme inline` in `app/globals.css`. The band is a surface, not a theme: no `.dark` class, no `dark:` variant, no `oklch()` — `tests/constraints.test.ts` refuses all three. The CLI's `init` and `add` may write an `oklch()` palette, a `.dark` block, `@import "shadcn/tailwind.css"`, a circular `--font-sans: var(--font-sans)` or the `shadcn` / `next-themes` packages into the project; delete them. The state variants the components are written in (`data-open`, `data-checked` …) are copied into `globals.css`, so the `shadcn` package is not a runtime dependency.
- **Restyle checklist** for a freshly added component: strip `border` and `ring-1 ring-foreground/10` from surfaces (cards are Card tiles with `shadow-card` or a tone; popovers, dialogs, sheets, dropdowns, tooltips and toasts carry `shadow-pop`); strip `outline-none` and `focus-visible:ring-*` from controls so the global `:focus-visible` outline shows (fields and select triggers keep `outline-none` and use the border + glow; menu items keep `outline-hidden` because the highlight is the focus); rename `font-heading` to `font-display` on titles and tab triggers; replace `bg-black/*` and `backdrop-blur` on overlays with `bg-ink/40`; retime `animate-in` / `animate-out` to `duration-160`–`280` with `ease-(--ease)` / `ease-(--ease-out)`; keep every colour a semantic class — never a hex, never `bg-[rgb(…)]`. Leave the structure, the a11y wiring and the `data-slot` attributes intact so `shadcn diff` stays tractable.
- **Three breakpoints, two spellings.** `560 / 900 / 1080` are `max-phone: / max-cols: / max-forms:` on the landing page and `sm: / md: / lg:` (min-width) inside `components/ui/`; there is no `xl` or `2xl`, and `hooks/use-mobile.ts` switches at the 900 line, not shadcn's 768.
- **Icons.** Landing page icons stay the authored sprite in `components/IconDefs.tsx`. `lucide-react` is permitted for the dashboards only — and inside the dashboard-only components that ship with it (dialog, sheet, select, dropdown-menu, breadcrumb, sidebar, sonner) — at `strokeWidth={2}` and 20px to match the existing `#i-yes` / `#i-no` marks. `PhotoTile`'s placeholder glyphs are authored in the same style. No emoji, no icon fonts.
- **Never ported to Radix.** The FAQ stays native `<details>` (the answers exist in the DOM without JavaScript; Radix `Accordion` hides them) and the Patients / GPs switch stays two real `<a href>` links with `aria-current` — `components/ui/segmented-link.tsx`, never `Tabs` or `ToggleGroup`. If a dashboard needs an accordion, install shadcn's for it and leave `Faq.tsx` alone.
- **Gallery.** `/dev/ui` (`app/dev/ui/`) renders every installed component in every variant and state on the ground, on white, on the forest band and on lime. It is the screenshot target for parity checks and for dashboard work; it 404s in a production build and `/dev/*` carries the noindex header. Keep it current when a component is added or restyled.

## Blog and admin (2026-09-25)

A public blog at `/blog` written by named admins in a Markdown editor at `/admin/blog`. No comments.

- **Storage.** `lib/blog-store.ts`, in the `blog_posts` table (slug unique). Development with no `DATABASE_URL` uses PGlite in `.data/pglite`; tests use an in-memory PGlite (`tests/helpers/db.ts`); a production server with no database has no blog (reads empty, writes `store_unavailable`). Public reads never throw.
- **Rendering.** `components/blog/Prose.tsx` (react-markdown + remark-gfm) is the one renderer for posts, the editor preview and the legal pages. Raw HTML never renders (`script-src` keeps `'unsafe-inline'`); images render only from `/assets/` (the CSP's `img-src 'self'`); a Markdown heading 1 becomes an h2. Every post ends with the standing "general information, not medical advice" notice and real `tel:999` / `tel:111` links. `/blog` and `/blog/[slug]` revalidate every 300s and on every admin change; `app/sitemap.ts` lists the public pages and published posts.
- **Admins.** `ADMIN_USERS` holds `email|Display Name|scrypt:16384:<salt>:<hash>` entries separated by `;` — make one with `npm run admin:hash -- you@example.com "Your Name"`. Colons, never `$`: Next expands `$NAME` inside `.env` files. `ADMIN_SESSION_SECRET` (32+ characters) signs the `dq_admin` cookie (httpOnly, SameSite=Strict, path `/admin`, 8 hours); every read re-checks the admin is still listed, so removing an entry signs them out. Sign-in is throttled to five attempts per ten minutes per IP and email, and a wrong password and an unknown email get the same answer and the same scrypt cost. `proxy.ts` only redirects a request with no cookie; `requireAdmin()` in `lib/admin-auth.ts` is the real check, in the panel layout and at the top of every server action — keep it there in any new one.
- **Compliance gate.** `lib/compliance.ts` is the one rule set: the medicine list, the banned phrases, the patient-surface patterns and `PUBLIC_COPY_RULES` for prose. The editor shows `checkCopy()` hits live, and `publishPostAction` re-runs it on the server and refuses on any hit; drafts can still be saved. Add a rule there and it applies to source, pages and posts at once. It is the one file the source scans exclude, because it has to name what it bans.
- **CSP.** Unchanged: server actions submit by `fetch` after hydration (`connect-src 'self'`); `form-action 'none'` blocks only a no-JS submit, which the admin does not support.
- **Local use.** `.env.local` (gitignored) with an `ADMIN_USERS` entry and a session secret; posts persist in `.data/pglite`.

## Patient surface (the find-a-GP flow, 2026-09-23)

A fixture-driven prototype of the patient app at `/patient/**` (route group `app/(app)/patient`): the business plan's Uber-style request, where a patient asks for a GP, sees a live queue and is matched rather than booking a slot. It is noindexed twice (the `/patient/:path*` header and the layout's `metadata.robots`), carries the ribbon on every screen, ships blank (`?data=seeded` is the returning patient) and is never linked from the landing page.

- **Routes.** `/patient` (home); `/patient/book/[[...step]]`, ten steps and six states, each a static URL (`dynamicParams = false`; the bare route must be `{ step: [] }` in `generateStaticParams`, because `{}` throws E618); `/patient/consultations`; `/patient/consultations/[id]` (`dynamicParams = true`, because a consultation this session creates has no build-time page, which is also why this site cannot move to `output: 'export'`); `/patient/prescriptions`; `/patient/account`.
- **State.** `lib/booking-flow.ts` is the booking as a pure reducer in the `lib/session.ts` pattern: a `nav` counter, and an illegal action returns the same object. `components/patient/BookingProvider.tsx` mounts it in the layout. The URL follows the reducer (`router.replace` when `nav` bumps, on the booking route only). A URL naming another screen makes the booking jump there, unless a consultation is live (a hold authorised or captured), in which case the URL is put back. **A URL can never move money.** `PatientProvider` holds what the session produced (history, prescriptions, the ID check, the practice, the sharing preference) in memory only, never in storage, a cookie or the URL; `lib/patient.ts` derives every list from it and the fixtures. The practice it remembers comes from `booking.answered`, which only a valid NHS GP submit sets, so a URL landing is never an answer.
- **The price.** `lib/pricing.ts`: the plan's base of £32, plus £4 for each place in the queue beyond the first (read from `waitEstimate`, the same figure the queue shows), capped at £48. The step and the cap are prototype placeholders: `PRODUCT.md` records the floor, ceiling and multiplier as undecided. The quote is computed once, on reaching the quote screen, and frozen in `booking.quote`. The finding and ready screens and the receipt read that field and nothing else, and the home never shows a price. Blank mode is the first patient on an empty floor (£32, one place, 45 seconds); seeded is `FLOOR` (£40, three places, 135 seconds). The hold is explicit state: authorised on confirm, captured at the match, released in full on cancel or when no GP is available. "Try again" after no GP is a new request, quoted afresh.
- **Clocks.** The queue and the call run on wall-clock timestamps through `useLiveInterval`, so a hidden tab neither stretches nor stalls them. The per-second tick pauses while the tab is hidden, but the queue's end is a timeout of its own, which a background tab still fires, so the match lands on time while the patient is in another tab. The match interrupts with the ready screen when it is in view; when the patient has looked away it adds a sonner toast and a `<title>` of its own kept first in `<head>`, which is what a patient in another tab sees. Never assign `document.title` here: Next re-renders the metadata title after a navigation and the assignment could not be restored.
- **Screens.** Every booking screen is a `FlowStep`, with three layouts. `split` is the m.uber.com arrangement: a step panel beside a canvas showing the live status of the request, which sits under the heading on a phone. `column` is for screens that end something. `band` is for 999 only. Each screen has one docked action (`ActionDock`, primary first), never a draggable sheet. Cancel and End call are confirmed in a `Dialog`. The in-screen shortcuts (skip the wait, a declined card, no GP online) are `ProtoNote` and show only under the state jumper's rule.
- **Blank landings invent nothing.** A blank-mode URL landing on ready, call, outcome, done or ended-early renders `NothingYet`: no GP, no clock, no outcome, and a way to start. A URL never writes history; only a consultation the patient finished says "Saved to your account".
- **Navigation.** `PatientNav` renders one nav in two places: a row in the top bar from 560px, and the app's tab bar below that. It is hidden for the whole booking flow, and the home shows a resume card while a consultation is live, so a patient who wanders off can never abandon a hold by starting again.
- **Copy the patient surface must never carry** (tests/constraints.test.ts scans `app/(app)/patient`, `components/patient` and the three patient `lib/` modules, comments included): time pressure or scarcity (busy, hurry, act now, limited time, "N GPs online"); bought priority (priority, jump or skip the queue); a price range or floor (from £, up to £); prescription promises or medicine names (guarantee, prescriptions included); UK-wide availability; "is recorded"; CQC; urgency or triage scores; a named doctor; and a literal `£` figure in source (money reaches the screen only through `money()` and the frozen quote). What it must carry: the price in full before commit, what it covers, "the pharmacy charges separately", the hold sentence, a real `tel:999` on every booking screen before the call and at body size, `tel:111` when no GP is available, and "This call is not recorded."
- **Ratings (2026-10-01).** The matched-GP card shows the GP's rating in seeded mode only: a first patient is on a floor where nobody has rated anyone, so their GP shows none. The receipt asks for one to five stars after a consultation the patient really finished, never on a URL landing. The answer is held on the screen; a real flow hands it to `recordRating()` in `lib/ratings.ts`.
- **Open decisions, for a human.** Whether a call that ends early is refunded; whether a quote expires; children and parental responsibility (no age check exists); pharmacy nomination (a first patient's prescription waits with "No pharmacy saved yet"); the real price floor, ceiling and multiplier, and how the patient's price relates to what the GP is paid (`OFFER.fee`). Do not decide these in code.

## Forms

The two patient forms (hero, closing band) POST JSON to `/api/waitlist`:

```json
{ "email": "...", "role": "patient", "source": "hero" | "recap",
  "visitorId"?, "utmSource"?, "utmMedium"?, "utmCampaign"?, "referrer"?, "landingPath"? }
```

GPs do not use that route (it refuses the role): see "GP sign-up, fee and commission" below.

The attribution keys come from `getAttribution()` in `lib/analytics/track.ts`; `visitorId` is present only with analytics consent. The endpoint (`app/api/waitlist/route.ts`) stores the sign-up in Postgres (`lib/waitlist.ts`, Drizzle). It deliberately does **not** retain IP — the rate-limit key is a salted SHA-256 hash with a 600s TTL, never written into the waitlist record. Retrieve the list with `GET /api/waitlist-export` and a `WAITLIST_EXPORT_TOKEN` bearer token; without that variable set, the route refuses every request.

Responses the client handles: `200 {ok,alreadyJoined}`, `400` invalid, `429` rate limited, `503` store unconfigured, `502` write failed. Keep the honeypot field (`.hp`), client-side email validation, the distinct `.ok` / `.err` status states, and the focus move to the status region on success. Zero friction for a patient: email is the only field, ever.

## GP sign-up, fee and commission (2026-10-01)

Three decisions by the user, recorded in `PRODUCT.md` and in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-01-gp-signup-fee-and-commission-design.md`.

- **The sign-up is a pop-up that ends in a payment.** `GpSignupCta` (the GP hero and closing band) and the nav CTA carry `data-gp-signup`; `GpSignupDialog`, mounted once in `app/page.tsx`, listens for clicks on that attribute and for a `#gp-join` hash, and opens the one pop-up: a forest cell with the commission ladder, and a white cell with `GpSignupForm` (name, email, mobile, GMC number), the fee and one button. It is only ever opened by the reader, never on a timer, on scroll or on leaving. The fee and its refund sentence sit directly above the button, and are also stated beside every trigger before the click.
- **The flow.** The form POSTs to `/api/gp-signup`, which validates (the same `lib/gp-signup` rules as the client), stores the application with `fee_status = 'unpaid'` (`startGpSignup`), creates a Stripe Checkout Session (`lib/stripe.ts`) and answers `{ ok, url }`; the browser goes to Stripe's hosted page. Stripe returns to `/gp/welcome?session_id=…` or, without paying, to `/?checkout=cancelled#gp-join`. Other answers: `{ ok, alreadyPaid }`, `400 invalid_<field>`, `429`, `503 payments_unavailable` (no key: nothing stored), `502 checkout_failed`.
- **Whose details.** Anyone can type anyone's email, so a form post never rewrites an existing application: the row keeps the first details submitted for an address, and details typed later are kept beside the checkout they started (`pending_details`, `recordFeeCheckout`) and replace the row's only if that checkout is paid. Do not "fix" this into an upsert.
- **One open checkout per sign-up.** Starting a new one expires the previous one, and each expires after an hour. Three rate limits guard the route: per client, per email address named, and a shared bucket for requests with no client address.
- **Paid is decided by Stripe, never by the browser.** `app/api/webhooks/stripe` handles `checkout.session.completed` and `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded` and marks the fee paid only when the session is paid, for the exact fee, in pounds (`lib/gp-fee-store.ts`, idempotent). A second payment for a paid sign-up, or a payment for a sign-up that no longer exists, is written to `app_errors` for the team to refund. Events from a connected account or from the other mode than the key are ignored. The welcome page reads the session back from Stripe and runs the same idempotent write, so it is true on arrival; the webhook is the path that cannot be missed. The confirmation email and the team alert are sent once, by whichever call recorded the payment. `refund.*` and `charge.refunded` mark a fully refunded fee `refunded`.
- **The fee** is `lib/gp-fee.ts`: the amount in pence, the label formatted from it, and `GP_FEE_REFUND`, the refund promise. That sentence is a commitment made next to a payment button: use the constant, never paraphrase it, and never add a promise the team does not keep by hand (refunds are made in the Stripe Dashboard; nothing is automatic).
- **Commission** is `lib/finance/commission.ts`: 60% to the GP, 70% after 100 completed consultations, 75% after 500. Every surface that states it (hero, pay tile, FAQ, pop-up, pricing page, admin payouts) reads that module; the demo finance data is split by it. When consultations are written for real, split the price with `splitPrice(price, servedBefore)`.
- **Stripe.** The SDK (`stripe`) is used only in `lib/stripe.ts`, server-side; the webhook's signature check is hand-rolled and predates it. No Stripe script runs in the browser, so the CSP is unchanged. Only the email and the sign-up id go to Stripe. Never pass `payment_method_types`. Setup and testing: `docs/stripe.md`.
- **Admin.** The GPs page shows fees collected, started-not-paid and refunded; each application carries a fee chip and, once paid, a link to the payment in Stripe. Finance → Payouts shows each GP's share and how far they are from the next tier.
- **Open, and not to be decided in code:** whether a GP who withdraws gets the fee back, VAT on the fee, and whether commission is taken before or after Stripe's charges (`PRODUCT.md`).

## Doctor portal (2026-10-01)

`/doctor` is where an approved GP works: sign in, go online, take the consultation they are offered, see what they have earned, edit their profile and see their rating. It is real and database-backed. It is not the fixture prototype of the same name that `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-02-doctor-admin-surfaces.md` planned and never mounted; that plan's pure modules (`lib/session.ts`, `lib/onboarding.ts`, `lib/earnings.ts`) are still in `lib/` and are not what the portal runs on. The design is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-01-doctor-portal-design.md`; the product decisions are in `PRODUCT.md`.

- **Nothing creates a consultation in production yet.** The patient surface is still a prototype, so a live server has no offers, earnings or ratings until a real patient flow exists. Locally: `npm run dev`, then `npm run db:seed:doctor` (a doctor who can sign in, with a history, and a GP who has not claimed an account) and `npm run doctor:request` (one more waiting patient). Both call the dev-only route `app/dev/doctor`, a 404 in a production build, because local development runs on PGlite, which only the dev server's own process can open.
- **Accounts.** There is no registration form. A GP signs up and pays on the landing page; at `/doctor/register` they type that email and are sent a one-time link to choose a password (`lib/doctor/account.ts`: 60 minutes, single use, only its hash stored). The same link is the password reset. The form answers every address the same way and does its work in `after()`, once the response has gone, so neither the words nor the wait says who has signed up. The first use creates the `gps` row from the sign-up. A sign-up whose fee is unpaid or refunded cannot claim an account (anyone can type anyone's GMC number into the form for nothing, and `gps.gmc` is unique); one from before the fee can. If the email fails, the new link is withdrawn and the earlier one keeps working, and the admin is told.
- **Approval is the waitlist pipeline, and nothing else.** `setStatusAction` moves the account with the application (`lib/doctor/admin.ts`): Active lets the doctor go online and emails them; any other status takes them off the floor at once; Rejected ends their sessions. From the "Doctor portal" card on a GP's admin page the team can also pause and resume an approved doctor and send the link; each is audited by sign-up id. A doctor who is not approved yet can sign in and edit their profile, and nothing more. Rejecting a doctor, and erasing one, are refused while they are in a consultation: an offboarded doctor cannot sign in, so nobody could end it.
- **Sign-in.** `lib/doctor-auth.ts`: cookie `dq_doctor` (httpOnly, SameSite=Strict, path `/doctor`, 12 hours), signed with `DOCTOR_SESSION_SECRET` through `lib/signed-token.ts`, which the admin cookie uses too. Every read loads the `gps` row, so offboarding a doctor, or a password change (which bumps `session_epoch`), ends their sessions on the next request. `proxy.ts` only redirects a request with no cookie; `requireDoctor()` is the real check, in the portal layout, every page and every server action. Keep it there in any new one, and never take a doctor's id from the browser: an action acts for the signed-in doctor.
- **Offers** (`lib/doctor/dispatch.ts`). One waiting consultation is offered to one online doctor for 45 seconds; a decline or a timeout passes it on, never to the same doctor twice. Oldest request first, to the doctor available longest, and never by price. The fee quoted is `splitPrice()` at the doctor's tier when it is offered, and the accept writes that figure. A request older than fifteen minutes is never offered. There is no background worker: `dispatch()` runs from the portal's poll and from every shift action, under an advisory lock, with partial unique indexes as the backstop. **A real patient flow must call `dispatch()` too**, when it creates a consultation and from the patient's wait screen: if the only doctor holding an offer shuts their laptop, nothing else is left to expire it.
- **Three rules inside `dispatch.ts` and `shift.ts`.** One clock: the `now` passed in, written as `${now.toISOString()}::timestamptz`, never the database's `now()`. Inside a transaction, use `tx` only: on PGlite, reaching for the outer handle never returns. Every write a doctor makes is one guarded statement, and one that changes nothing is asked why and answers with a reason. The stored window is 48 seconds and the countdown 45, so a last-second click lands.
- **The shift** (`lib/doctor/shift.ts`): offline, idle, offer, consultation, or resting. After a missed offer or a finished consultation the doctor is out of the rotation until they tap "Back online"; a decline sends them to the back of the queue without a tap. A portal silent for fifteen seconds loses its offer and gets none; silent for two minutes, the doctor is offline.
- **The poll** is `POST /doctor/pulse`: a route handler, not a server action, because Next runs a client's actions one at a time and a slow poll would queue in front of Accept. It is the heartbeat, it runs the dispatcher, and it returns the state to show. It answers 401 rather than redirecting and never sets a cookie (that would re-render the page). `ShiftProvider`, in the portal layout, polls every 3 seconds (10 in a consultation, not at all offline) and stops while the tab is hidden: a doctor who cannot see the portal must not be offered a patient. A poll that cannot reach the server shows "Connection lost"; a click that cannot is a toast, never the framework's error screen, which would take the poll down with it.
- **Earnings** (`lib/doctor/queries/earnings.ts`) are read-only and use the admin's definitions (`lib/finance/model.ts`), so a doctor and the team see the same figures; a test holds the two together. A fee is earned by a completed consultation whose payment succeeded, and every earned penny is awaiting a payout, in one, or paid. Nothing here makes a payout or calls Stripe.
- **Ratings** (`lib/ratings.ts`, `lib/rating-rules.ts`): decided by the user on 2026-10-01, reversing "no ratings". One to five stars for a completed consultation, once, with no comment (free text would be health data). A doctor is shown their average, count and spread, and not which consultation a rating came from; but those figures move as each rating arrives, so a doctor could work it out, and no screen may tell a patient that a rating cannot be traced. A patient sees a GP's rating on the matched-GP card once five patients have given one, still with no name or face. `recordRating()` is the only writer, and nothing in production calls it yet. A figure never rounds up to a five the doctor does not have. Marketing pages still carry no ratings and no reviews.
- **Erasure.** `erasePerson` deletes a portal account that never took a consultation. One that did keeps its name and GMC number with the financial records and loses the sign-in, the address, the mobile and the profile. Where that line belongs is for whoever owns data protection to confirm (`PRODUCT.md`).
- **Not built:** the video call, clinical notes, creating a payout, Stripe Connect.
- **Tests.** `tests/doctor-*.test.ts`, `tests/ratings.test.ts`, `tests/admin-doctor.test.ts`, `tests/dev-seed-doctor.test.ts`. PGlite runs every transaction behind one mutex and cannot show a race, so the dispatcher under contention is `tests/doctor-postgres-integration.test.ts`: opt-in with `TEST_DATABASE_URL`, against a disposable Postgres, because it empties the doctor tables.

## Compliance constraints (from Docs/Dr_Quick_Research_Report.md — read it before big changes)

- **The GP sign-up fee and the commission are fixed figures, and the rule below is not about them.** They may be stated on GP surfaces, always from `lib/gp-fee.ts` and `lib/finance/commission.ts` and never typed by hand. The page head and the share card carry no figure of any kind (`tests/metadata.test.ts`).
- **Pricing is dynamic (decided 2026-09-04, by the user, reversing the research report).** Supply and demand set the price, Uber-style. No page may state a fixed consultation price, and the flat `FEE` constant is gone from `lib/fixtures.ts` — money is summed from what each consultation actually paid, never a count multiplied by a rate. What survives the reversal, because it is law rather than pricing strategy:
  - **The price must be shown in full before the patient commits, and must not move after.** Quote, then hold. Since April 2025 the CMA can fine up to 10% of global turnover under the DMCC Act, and drip pricing — a headline figure that grows on the way to checkout — is banned outright. Dynamic *between* bookings is defensible; dynamic *during* one is not.
  - **Never present price as time pressure.** The ASA ruled time-pressure pricing in medical-service ads "socially irresponsible" (Menwell/Juniper, 2026). "Busier than usual", "priority queue", countdowns against a price, and the word "surge" in patient-facing copy stay banned — `tests/constraints.test.ts` enforces the wording. The compliant expression of the model is the patient promise ("Your price in full, before you book") and the GP incentive ("Paid more when demand is high"), never a nudge to book now before it costs more.
  - **Never price clinical priority.** A patient must never pay to be seen sooner than a clinically more urgent one. Demand may move the price; it may not reorder the queue on ability to pay.
  - `Docs/Dr_Quick_Landing_Page_info.pdf` remains an anti-reference. Its "£82 Priority · busier than usual" breaks all three rules above and is still superseded — the pricing *model* was reinstated, its *presentation* was not.
- Never claim CQC registration before it exists — current wording is "CQC-registered clinical service at launch". Never use "operating under a CQC-registered clinical partner".
- Never name medicines or imply a consultation guarantees a prescription (POM advertising is illegal in the UK). Keep prescription wording conditional — "any prescription you need", never "prescriptions included".
- Keep the 999/A&E emergency disclaimer. It appears twice by design: a band directly under the hero, and in the footer. The band must stay at body size inside the patient flow, and "call 999" must stay a real `tel:` link in both places — someone using this page may need to tap it while shaking.
- England only at launch. Never imply UK-wide availability — Scotland (HIS), Wales (HIW) and Northern Ireland (RQIA) are three separate regulatory regimes, and all three must be named when the page lists what is not covered.
- Collect nothing beyond email on this page — no symptoms, no health information.
- **`consultations.reason` is health data.** It, the age band and the record-sharing answer are what a GP sees before accepting an offer. Only the local demo seed fills them. Nothing may write them for a real patient until the privacy notice is complete and the legal entity exists (the last bullet here).
- Never state or imply that the consultation price covers the cost of the medicine. It covers writing the prescription; the patient pays the pharmacy. Claiming otherwise is a DMCC drip-pricing exposure.
- Controlled-drug wording must stay scoped to **Schedule 2 and 3**. A blanket "controlled drugs" claim is broader than the constraint and would be false at launch.
- The CSV export escapes leading `= + - @` so a crafted address cannot execute as a spreadsheet formula. Keep that guard if you touch `csvCell`.
- The FAQ promises deletion "until launch and for no more than twelve months after that". That retention figure is a default, not a confirmed decision (`PRODUCT.md`, Explicitly undecided) — but the page makes the promise, so the store must honour it.
- **The privacy notice is incomplete and this blocks launch.** UK GDPR Art 13 requires the controller's registered name and address and a working privacy contact at the point of collection. The legal entity is undecided, so they cannot be written yet; a `DEPLOY / LEGAL` comment in `index.html` marks the spot. Do not let this page collect a real address until it is filled in.
